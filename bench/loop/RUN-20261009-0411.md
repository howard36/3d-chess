### Bet 1: keep the tower's pieces at rest in a copy, sample for sample

Frame at rest = garden (already a copy) + every piece's glaze (~100 ms of ~130 ms in the last profile) + the see-through layers. A move changes one or two pieces; the shimmer of a last move asks for a frame every frame forever, and both move rows land in that state. Plan: the game canvas draws into its own 4-sample framebuffer (context without antialiasing, resolved onto the canvas each render), so colour *and* depth can be saved and restored by blitFramebuffer, exactly; frames at rest start from the copy, draw only the pieces that changed (plus a scissored repair of the rect a changed piece left) and everything see-through.
Drop it if: (a) the backbuffer path is not byte-identical to the canvas's own antialiasing at rest (more than edge pixels by 1), or (b) a cached frame does not save at least 30% of the frame at rest in the prototype, or (c) by 06:15Z it is not passing unit + e2e.

### 04:50–05:08Z — baseline; bet 1 dies

- Baseline on bf2c46d + the bench fix, 5 browser runs + client tier 5 rounds (04:27–04:51Z): scoreboard. Render section: an idle new game draws 0 frames yet the GPU process runs at 380% CPU; an idle game with a last move draws at 1 fps (531 ms apart); with reduced motion at 7.5 fps (should be 0).
- Bet 1, exact form (own 4-sample backbuffer, colour + depth saved and restored by blitFramebuffer): **impossible in WebGL2**. ES 3.0 forbids a multisampled *draw* framebuffer in blitFramebuffer, so the restore silently did nothing: the prototype's "15–18 ms" cached frames drew over the previous frame (glass blended twice: 320 k bytes differed, max 93). The reviewer's claim and my assumption were both wrong.
- Bet 1, depth-only form (resolved colour copy after the static pieces, their depth drawn again each frame with a colour-less twin of their vertex shader, one program): cached frames 92–98 ms vs 124–133 ms in full (−25%), repair frames 101–109 ms; 6.3–6.7 k bytes of 4.1 M differ, max 52 (silhouette pixels: glass and marks behind a piece blend over the pixel's resolved colour instead of the garden's samples). Fails both drop criteria (≥ 30% saved; edges by ≤ 1). **Dropped.** Patch: `patches/tower-depth-only.patch`. Re-rasterising the pieces is about half their cost; nothing exact restores per-sample depth into a multisampled canvas in WebGL2.
