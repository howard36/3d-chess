The first-pick fix moves the warm-up’s cost rather than removing it: take it anyway? (reverted, patch kept)

In software rendering the warm-up of the marks of play costs about 2 s of GPU time, and it lands on whatever the player does first after the entrance: on main, the first pick (about 2.4 s). W1b linked those programs under the entrance and read them back once the board rested. The first pick dropped to 0.8–1.05 s (called better in three A/Bs), but the second and third picks and the computer’s second reply each waited about 0.3 s more, and the primary “Selecting a piece” row (the median of 8 picks) leaned slower twice on the final code: +41% (0.95 1.58 1.43 1.85) and +37% (1.02 1.95 1.29), neither called. The run’s rule is never more than 3% slower, and CLAUDE.md calls buying one wait with another a trade-off, not a win, so I reverted it (20bb91f, 50bc357). It is in bench/loop/patches/warm-under-entrance-W1b.patch.

Recommendation Not as it is. Next run: spread the 13 read-backs over idle moments (W3), so no single action pays for them; with that, W1b should take the first pick’s 1.6 s without moving it elsewhere. If the first pick matters more to you than picks 2–3, apply the patch as is.

The join’s wait: fewer lobby frames while the board links? (design call)

A CPU profile of a join shows the joiner’s long tasks are getUniforms read-backs inside render(), each waiting for the GPU to finish the lobby frames queued before it (0.66, 1.15, 0.53 s), plus 0.3 s creating the board’s WebGL context. Linking ahead (tried, rejected) moves links, not those waits. What would shorten them is drawing fewer lobby frames while the board’s canvas starts: the arrival’s ring and lift would step instead of glide in software.

Recommendation Let me try a lower frame rate for the lobby only while the board’s first frame is pending, behind a measurement of its frames; you judge the look.
