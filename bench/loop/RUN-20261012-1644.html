Keep the first-pick fix? It moves the warm-up’s cost, it does not remove it

In software rendering the warm-up of the marks of play costs about 2 s of GPU time, and it lands on whatever the player does first after the entrance. On main that is the first pick (about 2.4 s). This branch links the programs under the entrance and reads them back once the board rests: the first pick drops to about 0.8 s, but the second and third picks and the computer’s second reply each wait about 0.3 s more. Net, the player waits about 0.8 s less over the first few actions; nothing is called worse on the final code (the computer’s reply +46% and selecting +41% lean worse, not called; an earlier version made the reply +114%, called, and was fixed). On a real GPU these links take milliseconds and none of this shows. No row times the entrance’s smoothness (T16).

Recommendation Keep it: the first pick was the one wait of seconds, and the rest are fractions. If you prefer the cost all in one place, revert 0fe7a3c + e4ee30c and keep the one-frame warm-up (924cb6f). Next run: spread the read-backs over idle moments (W3).

The join’s wait: fewer lobby frames while the board links? (design call)

A CPU profile of a join shows the joiner’s long tasks are getUniforms read-backs inside render(), each waiting for the GPU to finish the lobby frames queued before it (0.66, 1.15, 0.53 s), plus 0.3 s creating the board’s WebGL context. Linking ahead (tried, rejected) moves links, not those waits. What would shorten them is drawing fewer lobby frames while the board’s canvas starts: the arrival’s ring and lift would step instead of glide in software.

Recommendation Let me try a lower frame rate for the lobby only while the board’s first frame is pending, behind a measurement of its frames; you judge the look.
